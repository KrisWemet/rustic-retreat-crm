# Wedding access and roles

The CRM has four account roles: admin, client, staff, and family. Every new Supabase Auth account gets a client profile, but **no wedding access** until an admin links it. Clients cannot insert or update `public.users`, change `role`, or attach themselves to a booking. An admin links an existing, email-verified account from the wedding's **Wedding portal access** panel by email; the database rejects unverified email addresses. Both partners register and are linked separately as **Couple** to the same booking; staff and family are linked only to the weddings they help with. Existing `bookings.client_user_id` links are backfilled as couple memberships where the linked account was a client.

The public `/portal` route now verifies the signed-in role. An unlinked account sees a pending-access message. The `/admin` route remains admin-only. A couple can select only its own booking row and see a limited portal view of that wedding. Staff and family cannot select full booking rows because those contain package prices and overage amounts. Their portal data comes from `get_my_weddings` and `get_my_wedding_operations`, which expose dates and operational details without prices, payment items, receipts, damage deposits, contracts, activity, or inspection photos. Staff and family may complete readiness checks through `save_wedding_ops_check`; couples can view them but not change them.

Admin-only functions manage membership. An admin cannot assign one account different roles across different weddings; separate accounts are required. Removing the last membership removes wedding access, though its account role remains until another admin assignment. Admin accounts are never attached as portal members. The first admin must be bootstrapped by a trusted database/service-role operation after its Auth account exists; browser signup cannot promote an admin.

The policy/grant checks were run in PostgreSQL with separate simulated admin, two partner, staff, family, and outsider JWT identities. They verify role-update denial, cross-wedding isolation, and staff financial isolation.
